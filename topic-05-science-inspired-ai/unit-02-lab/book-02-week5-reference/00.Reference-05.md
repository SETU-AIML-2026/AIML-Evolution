# Week 5 Reference Answers

**Open this after you have uploaded `SUBMISSION.md`.** Go through it beside your own sheet.

Every page has, for each question: the **reference answer** with the numbers from a reference
run; **why**, in a few sentences; and for the predictions, **the tempting answer**: the prediction
people most often make, and what makes it tempting. A wrong prediction you can now explain is worth
more than a right one you guessed.

Your numbers may differ from these in the third decimal place. That is normal: the pattern is the
result.

## How to use the gaps

For each question where the reference explains something differently from you, write **one line
in your own words** in your notes: not the reference's sentence, yours. Those lines are the ones
that stick, and they are exactly what the Week 6 quiz asks about.

## The six predictions people get wrong most often

| Question | The tempting answer | What actually happens |
|---|---|---|
| **1e** | With perfect measurements, data-only finally beats the law | It still loses, about 4 times: its error is between the points, not in them |
| **3a** | Physics with the same data degrades "a bit" beyond the data | It does not degrade at all (1.0) |
| **4a** | Weight 500 is best, because the start matters most | Weight 500 is the worst by far, and it still gets the start right |
| **4e** | More checkpoints means a smaller loss | The smallest loss comes with the *largest* error |
| **4g** | A law 20% wrong is still much better than no law | It keeps about a quarter of the advantage; at 2x wrong it is already worse than no law |
| **5c** | Enough data will rescue the symmetric model | No amount can: it cannot see height at all |
