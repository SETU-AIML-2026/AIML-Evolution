## What you are doing

The lecture said: **the world is the first dataset, and it is not fair.** Here you look
at the loan file before any model is trained. Each person has seven "permitted" facts
(income, savings, postcode score, ...) and an outcome column `repaid`.

## Run this

The first cell of the notebook loads everything. The second is Step 1:

```python
df = lab["df"]
for name, on in df.attrs["mechanisms"].items():
    print(f"  [{'x' if on else ' '}] {name}")

print(df.groupby("group").latent.mean().round(3))      # true ability, by group

h.population_table(df)[["group", "n", "share", "repaid_rate", "mean_income",
                        "mean_savings", "mean_credit_history_months",
                        "mean_postcode_score"]].round(3)
```

## What you should see

- Four `[x]` marks: all four bias mechanisms are switched on.
- Two "true ability" numbers that are almost identical (about `-0.017` and `-0.005`).
  **That is the proof the groups are equal.**
- A table like this:

| group | n | share | repaid_rate | mean_income | mean_savings | mean_credit_history_months | mean_postcode_score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 6372 | 0.796 | 0.547 | 31,840 | 4,197 | 83.5 | 0.717 |
| B | 1628 | 0.204 | 0.437 | 25,756 | 2,333 | 53.6 | 0.064 |

On paper, group B is a fifth of the file, earns less, has saved less, has a shorter
credit history, and repays eleven points less often. In reality B is equal. Everything
"on paper" was put there by the mechanisms.
