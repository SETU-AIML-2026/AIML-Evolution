# The Metrics, Written Out

`fairness.py` implements every criterion in booklet Chapter 8 in numpy. No
Fairlearn, no AI Fairness 360. The point is that once you have seen how short
they are, *"the tool says the model is fair"* stops being an acceptable answer.

```python
def group_metrics(y, s, t, label):
    yhat = (s >= t).astype(int)              # the threshold IS the decision
    tp, fp, fn, tn = confusion(y, yhat)
    return GroupMetrics(
        selection_rate = _safe_div(tp + fp, len(y)),   # demographic parity
        tpr            = _safe_div(tp, tp + fn),       # equal opportunity
        fpr            = _safe_div(fp, fp + tn),       # equalised odds
        ppv            = _safe_div(tp, tp + fp),       # predictive parity
    )
```

That is the whole of Chapter 8. Four divisions.

## The one detail worth knowing

`_safe_div` returns `nan` rather than `0.0` when a denominator is empty.

Returning zero would be a lie that flatters the model: a group with no positive
cases would appear to have a perfect false-negative rate. You will meet this in
real audits at intersections, where counts get small — which is exactly where
Gender Shades found the largest effects.

Report the counts alongside the metrics, always, so a reader can see where the
estimates are unreliable.

## The baseline model, at the library default

`train_model()` fits a gradient-boosted classifier on the seven permitted
features. `group` is never given to it. At `t = 0.5`:

| group | n | base rate | selection | accuracy | TPR | FPR | PPV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2230 | 0.548 | 0.596 | 0.706 | 0.776 | 0.378 | 0.713 |
| B | 570 | 0.437 | 0.393 | 0.668 | 0.570 | 0.255 | 0.634 |
| **ALL** | 2800 | 0.525 | 0.554 | **0.699** | 0.741 | 0.348 | 0.702 |

And the four gaps:

```
demographic parity gap (selection rate) : 0.203
equal opportunity gap  (TPR)            : 0.205
false-positive rate gap                 : 0.122
predictive parity gap  (PPV)            : 0.079
```

## What to notice

The aggregate accuracy is **0.699**. It is the only number most projects report,
and it conceals a twenty-point selection gap and a twenty-point gap in the rate
at which creditworthy people are recognised.

Look at the TPR column specifically. A group-A applicant who would have repaid is
approved 77.6% of the time. An identical group-B applicant is approved 57.0% of
the time. Same latent creditworthiness, by construction, twenty points of
difference in whether the system notices.

Now look at FPR. Group B's false-positive rate is *lower* — 0.255 against 0.378.
On a punitive decision that would be the group being treated better. On a
lending decision it means group B is being refused more aggressively at every
level of risk, which is the same fact wearing a different sign.

> Which of those four gaps is the harm depends on what the decision does to a
> person. That is a question about the world, not about the code, and Chapter 8
> says you answer it in writing before you tune anything.
