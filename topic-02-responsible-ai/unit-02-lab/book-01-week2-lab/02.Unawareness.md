## Read the table carefully

The obvious conclusion is *"postcode is the problem, delete it"*. Hold that
thought until page 7, where you will delete it and the disparity will get
**worse**.

The reason is in the small numbers. Postcode carries most of the signal, but it
does not carry all of it. Income, savings and credit history each carry a
little, because `historical_bias` put it there — those columns record access,
not ability. Remove the loudest proxy and a capable learner redistributes onto
the quiet ones.

> There is no single column to delete. That is not a claim about this dataset.
> It is a claim about any dataset with a few hundred features and a few thousand
> rows, and it is why Amazon abandoned the recruiting engine rather than patching
> it a second time.

## What you have lost, not gained

The second half of the Chapter 5 argument matters more than the first.

Having refused to record `group`, you cannot compute a single metric on the next
page. Every fairness criterion in Chapter 8 requires knowing who is in which
group. Unawareness did not remove the disparity. It removed your ability to see
it, and left the disparity exactly where it was.

This is the paradox Chapter 14 covers: GDPR Article 9 restricts the very data
you need to prove a model is fair, and the 2026 Omnibus amendment exists because
that was blocking the measurement.
