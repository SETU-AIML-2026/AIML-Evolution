## Four things to take from it

**Dropping the obvious proxy makes the disparity worse.** The selection gap goes
from 0.2025 to 0.2731, and the false-positive gap nearly doubles. The model
leans harder on the proxies that remain, and it loses a point and a half of
accuracy for the privilege. There is no single column to delete — page 2 told you
why, and this is the receipt.

This is the single most useful result in the lab, because dropping the column is
what almost every team tries first, and it is worse than doing nothing.

**Reweighting helps, costs a little accuracy, and quietly worsens something
else.** Selection and FPR gaps come down substantially. The predictive-parity gap
goes *up*, from 0.0792 to 0.1260. Nobody mentions that in the standup, because
nobody was looking at that column.

**Per-group thresholds zero whichever gap you tuned for and inflate the others.**
That is Chapter 9 in production form. They also require using the protected
attribute at decision time, which in the EU edges into direct discrimination —
far harder to justify than the indirect kind. Legally the riskiest row in the
table, and the one most commonly shipped, because it is the latest and cheapest
to implement.

**Fixing the label collapses nearly every gap at an accuracy cost of 0.0007.**
Not 0.007. Seven ten-thousandths. Every gap lands between 0.008 and 0.033.

## Why the last row wins

Because stage three is where the harm was introduced.

`label_bias` recorded some group-B borrowers who repaid as having defaulted.
Every downstream disparity — the model's, the threshold's, the explanation's —
was that one fact propagating. Repair it at source and there is nothing left for
the later stages to amplify.

Notice the geometry against the seven-stage pipeline. Post-processing patches
stage six for a problem created at stage three. It is the most common
intervention because it is the latest and cheapest to implement, and it is the
least effective for exactly the same reason.

> **The cheapest real fix is always the earliest one.** On a real project you
> rarely get to rewrite the label. But you do get to notice, before training,
> that the column called `repaid` records institutional behaviour — and to write
> that sentence down where a reviewer will see it.

## The three families, in one table

| where | what it does | what it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-processing** | Reweight, resample or repair the training data | Model-agnostic and treats causes. But it alters data you may be required to retain unmodified, and it is hard to explain to an auditor |
| **In-processing** | Fairness constraints or penalties in the objective; worst-group objectives | The most principled place to act, and the trade is explicit in the loss. Needs control of training, and can be less stable |
| **Post-processing** | Adjust outputs or thresholds after the fact, per group | Works on models you cannot retrain, including vendor components. Frequently the legally riskiest option in the EU |
