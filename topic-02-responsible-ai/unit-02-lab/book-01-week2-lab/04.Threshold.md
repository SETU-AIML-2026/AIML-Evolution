## Figure 1: the two distributions

Two score distributions that overlap heavily but whose centres differ, with one
vertical line at 0.5 cutting across both.

Nothing about that line is unequal. It is the same rule, applied without regard
to group — the textbook definition of equal treatment. It produces a
twenty-point difference in selection rate, because the distributions it is
applied to differ.

The disparity is not in the rule. It arrived in stages 1 to 3 and the threshold
merely cashes it out.

## Figure 2: the sweep

Move the threshold from 0 to 1 and watch four gap curves. They do not move
together, and they do not have a common zero. Three things to look for:

- **Accuracy is flat across a wide middle range.** You can move the threshold a
  long way at almost no cost to the number in the report, while moving the
  fairness gaps a great deal. The accuracy-versus-fairness trade is much weaker
  than people assume near the default.
- **The selection gap and the PPV gap move in opposite directions.** Closing one
  opens the other. This is the impossibility result arriving early.
- **At the extremes everything looks fair.** Approve nobody and both groups have
  a selection rate of zero. A perfect parity gap. This is Exercise 2, and it is
  why a fairness number quoted without the selection rate is unreported rather
  than good.

## Figure 3: calibration

Predicted probability against observed frequency, per group. The curves sit
close to the diagonal and close to each other: the model is *well calibrated*.

A score of 0.7 means roughly a 0.7 chance of repayment for a group-A applicant
and roughly a 0.7 chance for a group-B applicant. The vendor in the COMPAS
dispute was making exactly this claim, and it was true.

Hold both facts at once: the score means the same thing in each group **and**
the error rates differ by twenty points. Page 5 explains why that is not a
contradiction, and not fixable.

## The lesson to carry

> **Not choosing a threshold is choosing 0.5.** The default in your library is a
> policy: adopted silently, by nobody, in a codebase with your name on the commit.

Sweep it. Produce the trade-off curve. Choose in a meeting, with a written record
and named participants. That is what Chapter 15's workflow means by step five,
and it is the part of the model card that carries the marks.
