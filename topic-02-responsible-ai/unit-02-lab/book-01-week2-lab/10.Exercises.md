# Exercises

1. **Isolate the mechanisms.** Regenerate the dataset with one mechanism at a
   time — `sampling_bias`, `historical_bias`, `proxy`, `label_bias` — and record
   which metric responds to each. Which produces the largest selection gap?
   Which produces the largest calibration error? *They are not the same
   mechanism, and the reason is the whole of Chapter 9.*
2. **Find the degenerate solution.** Find a threshold at which the
   demographic-parity gap is essentially zero. Report the selection rate too,
   and explain why any fairness result quoted without it is unreported rather
   than good. Name the group that this "fair" threshold serves worst.
3. **Break the explanation.** Explain one refused group-B applicant with all
   three methods and compute the rank correlation between the orderings. Decide
   which you would put in front of the refused applicant, and defend it. Then
   say what you would tell them if they asked whether the other two agreed.
4. **The Week 9 preview.** Retrain with `model="logreg"` and compare worst-group
   accuracy against the gradient-boosted model. Which is fairer, which is more
   accurate on aggregate, and would anyone reading only the headline number have
   been told? *This is the compression argument of Week 9 in miniature.*
5. **Write the model card.** Use the template. The fairness criterion, the
   reasoning behind it, and the cost stated in numbers from your own run are the
   graded parts.

## Going further, if you have time

- **Run the Chapter 5 experiment on real data.** Take any tabular dataset with a
  protected attribute and try to predict that attribute from the other columns.
  Ten minutes, and it is the single most persuasive demonstration you can put in
  front of a stakeholder who believes dropping a column solved the problem.
- **Test at an intersection.** Add a second protected attribute to the generator
  and evaluate at the intersection. Watch the counts fall and the confidence
  intervals widen. Gender Shades' finding lived at an intersection; either
  variable alone would have understated it.
- **Change the decision, not the model.** Implement a three-way output — approve,
  refuse, refer to a human — and measure what a referral band does to the gaps.
  This is Chapter 9's third escape, and several of the most effective real-world
  responses to this literature have been redesigns of the decision.

criteria), diagnostic (given a scenario, name the pipeline stage that failed and
