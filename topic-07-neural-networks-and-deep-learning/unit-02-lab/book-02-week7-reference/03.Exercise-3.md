# Exercise 3 · How it is wired

**3a · prediction.** ReLU(−3) = **0**, ReLU(0.5) = **0.5**, ReLU(2) = **2**. Negatives are blocked;
positives pass through unchanged.

**3b · build.** `return np.maximum(0, X @ W + b)`. The order matters: add the bias *before* the bend,
and multiply the dots by the weights (`X @ W`), not the other way round.

**3c · record and explain.** Answers **0, 1, 1, 0**: exactly XOR. The dots (0, 1) and (1, 0) both
become the hidden pair **(1, 0)**: the two orange dots land on the same spot, while the blues sit at
(0, 0) and (2, 1). In these new numbers one straight line separates orange from blue. The hidden
layer did not draw a curve; it moved the dots.

**3d · prediction.** Only **(0, 0)** goes wrong.
*The tempting answer:* every dot goes wrong. Three of the four hidden pairs never go negative, so the
bend changed nothing for them.

**3e · explain.** Without the bend, the second hidden neuron gives −1 for (0, 0), and the output
turns it into 0 − 2 × (−1) = **2**, which is wrong. More generally, without bends the whole network
is one straight-line formula, and XOR needs more than a straight line. The bend is what lets a
neuron switch off.

**3f · record and explain.** **337 dials.** Layer 1: 2 × 16 = 32 weights plus 16 biases = 48.
Layer 2: 16 × 16 = 256 weights plus 16 biases = 272. Output: 16 weights plus 1 bias = 17.
