# Exercise 1 · A formula with dials

**1a · prediction.** a = 2000 gives the smallest loss: zero. The other two are about **9,000**.
*The tempting answer:* about 900. The misses are tens of thousands of euro (60, 90 and 120 thousand),
and squaring numbers that size gives thousands, not hundreds.

**1b · record.** a = 1000: **8,700**. a = 2000: **0**. a = 3000: **8,700**.

**1c · explain.** With a = 1000 the misses are −60, −90 and −120; with a = 3000 they are +60, +90 and
+120. Same size, opposite sign. Squaring throws the sign away, so the two losses are identical: one
setting is exactly as wrong too low as the other is too high.

**1d · change (optional).** No. With a base price of €40k, a = 2000 now misses every house by €20k
and scores **400**. The best setting moves to **a = 2207**, with a loss of **28**. The two dials work
together: change one and the best value of the other moves. That is why learning turns all the
dials at once rather than one at a time.

**1e · sort.** Parameters: **a, b, and a neuron's weights**. Hyperparameters: **the step size, the
number of hidden layers and the number of epochs**. Test: does the computer change it during
training? If yes, it is a parameter.
