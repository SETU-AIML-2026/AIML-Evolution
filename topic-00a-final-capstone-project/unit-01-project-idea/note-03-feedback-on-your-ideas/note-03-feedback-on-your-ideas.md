## What we learned from all 26 ideas

The same few points came up again and again. Check your own idea against them.

1. **A law, not data.** Share prices, fuel prices and the timing of leaves changing all need records of the past. Your project learns from a law of science instead, so it needs none.
2. **The inputs are the law's own numbers.** If an input is a patient's weight, condition or other medicines, it needs data to link it to the law. Use the law's numbers instead (for a painkiller, the rate k), and say in your limits what you left out.
3. **No measurements, ever.** Nobody needs to make cups of tea. The "examples" for the comparison model are points taken from your exact formula or solver, made for you in the notebook.
4. **Check against the right thing.** Coffee, painkiller, camera flash, a slowing swing and a river spill have an exact formula. Big-angle pendulums, foxes and rabbits, and flu do not: use the solver, plus the free extra check where one exists (the energy for a pendulum, the total number of people for flu).
5. **Make success a number.** "Close" or "accurate" cannot be checked. "The peak day within one day of the solver" or "the drinkable time within one minute of the exact formula" can.
6. **Name a person.** "A café owner deciding when to serve" beats "the coffee industry".
7. **Timing, not medical advice.** For medicines, predict when the level drops below a threshold; deciding the dose is a clinician's job.
8. **Simple is not unambitious.** The marks reward a fair comparison, honest checks and a clear report, not a harder law.
9. **Sharing an idea is fine.** Several of you chose the same law. Your person, your numbers, your question and your work make it yours; the work is individual.
